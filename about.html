About Kurukshetra

The Land Of Dharma

Kurukshetra is an ancient city in Haryana, India. It is known around the world as the land of the Bhagavad Gita and has deep historical, religious and cultural importance.

History in Brief

Kurukshetra has a deep connection with the Mahabharata and the teachings of the Bhagavad Gita.

According to Indian tradition, the great battle of Mahabharata was fought in Kurukshetra between the Pandavas and Kauravas. It is believed that Lord Krishna shared the message of the Bhagavad Gita with Arjuna here. Today, Kurukshetra is known for its temples, sacred sarovars, museums, festivals and educational institutions.

Kurukshetra: The Land of Dharma

Kurukshetra is a historic, cultural and spiritual city in the Indian state of Haryana. It is often called Dharmakshetra, which means the land of righteousness. For many people, it is a place of faith, learning, reflection and pilgrimage.

Mahabharata and the Bhagavad Gita

Kurukshetra is closely connected with the Mahabharata. According to Hindu tradition, the great battle between the Pandavas and Kauravas took place here. Before the battle, Lord Krishna shared the teachings of the Bhagavad Gita with Arjuna, explaining duty, courage, right action and selfless service.

Jyotisar

Jyotisar is one of the most important spiritual sites in Kurukshetra. It is traditionally believed to be the place where Lord Krishna delivered the message of the Bhagavad Gita to Arjuna. Visitors come here to see the sacred banyan tree, Jyotisar Sarovar and displays related to the Gita.

Brahma Sarovar

Brahma Sarovar is one of the best-known landmarks of Kurukshetra. It is a large sacred water tank where visitors gather for prayer, peaceful walks, aarti and religious events. According to local belief, it has a connection with Lord Brahma and is an important place for holy bathing during special occasions.

Sannihit Sarovar

Sannihit Sarovar is another important sacred water site. It is respected by pilgrims and is visited during Amavasya, solar eclipses and other religious occasions. The sarovar represents the strong connection between water, faith and tradition in Kurukshetra.

Temples and Sacred Places

Kurukshetra has many places of worship and pilgrimage. Important sites include Sthaneshwar Mahadev Temple, Bhadrakali Temple, Sri Venkateswara Swamy Temple and several gurdwaras. These places show that Kurukshetra has a rich and welcoming religious heritage.

48 Kos Kurukshetra

The wider sacred region is known as 48 Kos Kurukshetra. It includes many tirthas, temples, sarovars and heritage sites connected with religious stories and local traditions. This shows that Kurukshetra’s heritage is not limited to one building or one area; it is spread across a large cultural landscape.

Museums and Cultural Learning

Visitors can learn more about Kurukshetra through places such as Sri Krishna Museum, Kurukshetra Panorama and Science Centre, Dharohar Museum and Mahabharata Anubhav Kendra. These places use objects, displays, art, stories and technology to explain the history, culture and traditions of the region.

Festivals and Traditions

Kurukshetra becomes especially vibrant during International Gita Mahotsav and Gita Jayanti celebrations. These events may include Bhagavad Gita recitations, cultural programmes, spiritual talks, exhibitions, Deep Daan and aarti at Brahma Sarovar. They bring together local people, pilgrims and visitors from different places.

Haryanvi Culture

The city also reflects the warm culture of Haryana. Local life includes traditional food, folk music, dance, family gatherings, hospitality and respect for elders. Kurukshetra gives visitors a chance to experience both ancient heritage and the everyday culture of Haryana.

Education and Modern Learning

Kurukshetra is not only known for religion and history; it is also an important centre of education. Kurukshetra University was established in 1956 and supports higher education and research. This creates a meaningful link between ancient knowledge and modern learning in the city.

Why Kurukshetra Is Important Today

Kurukshetra remains important because its message is still meaningful today. The teachings connected with the Bhagavad Gita encourage people to act responsibly, stay calm during difficult situations and make ethical choices. It is a place where heritage, spirituality, education and culture come together.

💡 Quick Fact

Kurukshetra is mentioned in the Mahabharata and is one of the most respected spiritual and historical places in India.
