Heritage of Kurukshetra

Explore the historical, religious and cultural traditions that make Kurukshetra special.

A Land of History, Faith and Culture

Kurukshetra is widely known for its connection with the Mahabharata and the teachings of the Bhagavad Gita. It is also a place where history, pilgrimage, local traditions and learning meet.

Historical Heritage

The region has links with ancient settlements, the Mahabharata tradition and later historical periods. Museums and archaeological sites help visitors explore these stories.

Kurukshetra has a long and layered history. Archaeological evidence shows that the wider region has connections with very old settlements. It is also closely connected with the Mahabharata tradition and the name of King Kuru.

Over time, Kurukshetra was influenced by different communities and rulers. These included ancient Indian dynasties and later Central Asian, Afghan and Mughal influences. This mixture helped shape the area’s history, buildings and local traditions.

Today, visitors can explore this history through museums, monuments and archaeological places. Sri Krishna Museum, Kurukshetra Panorama and Science Centre, Sheikh Chilli’s Tomb and Harsh Ka Tila are examples of places that help people understand the region’s past.

Religious Heritage

According to Hindu tradition, Kurukshetra is associated with the Bhagavad Gita. Places such as Jyotisar, Brahma Sarovar and Sannihit Sarovar attract pilgrims and visitors.

Kurukshetra is known as Dharmakshetra, meaning the land of righteousness. According to Hindu tradition, it is the place where Lord Krishna gave Arjuna the teachings of the Bhagavad Gita before the Mahabharata battle.

Jyotisar is especially important because it is traditionally recognised as the place connected with this event. Visitors also come to Brahma Sarovar and Sannihit Sarovar, where many people take part in prayer, worship and religious gatherings.

Other respected places include Sthaneshwar Mahadev Temple, Bhadrakali Temple and several temples within the 48 Kos Kurukshetra area. During important occasions such as Gita Jayanti and solar eclipses, the city receives many pilgrims from India and other countries.

Cultural Heritage

Kurukshetra brings together Haryanvi language, folk music, festivals, food, crafts and traditions influenced by many communities over time.

Kurukshetra’s heritage is not only found in monuments and sacred places. It is also present in the everyday life of local people through Haryanvi language, family values, food, music, folk dance and traditional crafts.

The region has been influenced by Hindu, Jain, Buddhist, Sikh and other cultural traditions over many centuries. This has created a rich mix of stories, customs and celebrations that continue to be shared by local communities.

The International Gita Mahotsav is one of the best-known cultural events in Kurukshetra. It includes devotional programmes, cultural performances, exhibitions and activities that share the message of the Bhagavad Gita with visitors.

💡Heritage Fact

Kurukshetra's 48 Kos area includes many pilgrimage places, temples and sacred water bodies.
